# 7. Activity of Day 7

## Digital Fabrication III: CNC Router Milling & Cutting

In Day 7, I worked with a 3-axis CNC router to machine the final CO3 nameplate from walnut hardwood. The process covered CAM toolpath preparation, machine setup, cutting execution, and final inspection.

#### Overview

CNC milling removes material from a workpiece using toolpaths generated from a digital design. For this activity, the machine followed a program that controlled the router on multiple axes to cut the nameplate accurately.

#### Safety

CNC operations require strict safety practices. I kept my hands away from moving tools, monitored the machine continuously, and confirmed that the workpiece was securely clamped before starting the cut.

#### CAM Toolpath Preparation

The design was imported into CAM software, and the cutting strategy was defined. Typical steps included roughing to remove bulk material, finishing to refine the letter surfaces, and profiling to cut the final part outline.

#### CNC Execution

The walnut blank was secured on the bed and the router was zeroed to the material surface. The machining sequence started with the roughing pass, followed by a finishing pass, and finished with the profile cut to free the nameplate.

#### Challenge

During the toolpath generation stage, a problem occurred when the CNC software attempted to create the cutting path. The toolpath was damaged, making the instructions unusable by the CNC machine. This issue was identified during preparation and required revising the machining setup before proceeding.

#### Inspection and Results

After machining, the part was removed, the tabs were trimmed, and the nameplate was inspected. The final piece showed accurate dimensions and clean letter details, demonstrating the CNC workflow from design to finished part.
